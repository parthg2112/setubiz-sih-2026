# PRODUCT.md

## Register

product. The design serves the advisory; it never performs. Every visual decision must make a number easier to trust or an action easier to take.

## Users

- **Rural micro-entrepreneur** (primary): low form literacy, often Hindi-first, frequently on a shared phone at a Common Service Centre. Reads slowly, distrusts fine print, decides on a few large numbers. Cannot decode financial jargon; can read a rupee amount.
- **VLE / bank-branch operator** (assisted mode): fills the form on someone's behalf, prints the sheet, needs the document checklist and the SCA address to be unmissable.
- **Ministry / jury evaluator**: audits provenance in seconds. Asks "where did this number come from?" of every figure on screen.

## Product purpose

A deterministic advisory that tells a rural applicant two numbers: the most the scheme formula permits them to borrow, and the most their cash flow can actually repay. The gap between those numbers is the product. Feasibility (six parameters), loan structuring, scheme routing and stress testing are computed from real government datasets; the language layer only explains what the engines decided.

## Tone

Government-grade, plain, unhurried. Short sentences. Rupee amounts in Indian grouping. Confidence stated in words ("Fairly sure", "Roughly right"). Honesty is a feature: ranges over points, documented gaps over invented data, refusals over bad approvals.

## Anti-references

- Fintech dashboards: gradient accents, hero-metric cards, dark-mode trading aesthetics. None of it.
- Chatbot wrappers: conversational filler, numbers without sources.
- Form walls: nine controls on one screen.

## Strategic principles

1. The answer leads. Justification follows on demand.
2. Every number carries its source within one interaction.
3. Colour never carries meaning alone; text and icons repeat it.
4. Progressive disclosure for reasoning, never for warnings or the answer.
5. Print is a first-class output: the sheet handed across a bank counter is the real deliverable.
